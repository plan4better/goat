# GOAT Core API

The main backend: who may do what, and the metadata of everything a user works with.

## Overview

Core owns the Postgres schema (`customer` by default) and serves it under `/api/v2`:

- **People and organizations**: users, organizations, teams, members and invitations, custom domains, organization analytics. A user belongs to one organization. Owners and admins manage every team of their organization (`GET /teams?scope=organization`, renaming, members, naming a new owner with `PUT /teams/{id}/owner`); `GET /teams` without it stays the caller's own teams, because the share dialogs read it. `GET /teams/{id}/members` answers only the team's members and the organization's owners and admins (anyone else gets null, as from `GET /teams/{id}`): its seed row lets every member of the organization through, so the handler narrows it. Teams are created from Bearbeiter (editor) up, since a team brings a space for content. A pending invitation can be sent again every 10 minutes (`POST /organizations/{id}/invitations/{invitation_id}/resend`, `RESEND_AFTER`); the limit lives in the invitation's payload, not in a per-process limiter, since core runs several pods. The organization's owner cannot delete their account while the organization exists (`DELETE /users` answers 409), since nobody could then delete or hand it over. `DELETE /organizations/{id}` suspends one and sets `deleted_at` (an entitlement push for it answers 409); `DELETE /organizations/{id}/permanent` (superusers) removes an organization that has at most one member and nothing in it, so that member can be invited elsewhere (`crud_organization.delete_permanently` lists what counts). Billing is not touched: a subscription stays in the billing system (Plan4Better's Odoo: under the organization's `odoo_company_id`, named in the response) until someone ends it there; its entitlement pushes find no organization and are refused.
- **Content and access**: spaces, folders, the unified content feed, trash and transfer, favourites, sharing grants.
- **Projects**: projects and their layers, layer groups, workflows, report layouts, publishing.
- **Datasets**: layer and bundle metadata, upload URLs, templates, user assets.
- **Quotas and billing**: organization quotas and extras, pushed by a billing system (`POST /webhooks/billing/entitlement`), and GOAT-managed trials (`POST /webhooks/trial`). Core knows no billing system: it stores and enforces what is pushed. The interface is in `docs/billing-interface.md`. Billing is a switch of its own (`settings.billing_enabled`: `BILLING_ENABLED` and `BILLING_WEBHOOK_SECRET`), independent of support and of Odoo; off, the credit routes and both webhooks answer 404, organizations get no trial and unlimited credits, and `GET /users/me/features` tells the web app to hide the Usage page. The trial's length and quotas and the credit rates are the billing system's terms, not settings: it pushes them (`POST /webhooks/billing/terms`), core keeps them in `billing_terms` and `credit_rate` and uses its own defaults (`crud/crud_billing_terms.py`) until the first push, so a sign-up never waits for the billing system. geoapi, processes and the workers read `BILLING_ENABLED` for the same switch.
- **AI credits**: the answers of GOAT AI (the assistant's own model) are counted in a pool per organization apart from the credits (`total_ai_credits`, `used_ai_credits`; NULL total, as with billing off, is unlimited). A trial gets the terms' trial amount; a subscription gets its editor seats times the terms' amount per editor, recomputed by every entitlement push that carries `total_editors` and by a terms push that changes the amount, unless the push sets `total_ai_credits` itself (a special deal, `ai_credits_override`). The pool resets with the credits. The assistant does not touch the tables: it calls two SQL functions in `src/core/db/sql/functions/billing/`, `ai_credits_left` before an answer and `charge_ai_answer` after it. They are `SECURITY DEFINER` with a pinned search path, so an assistant role that may only read the customer tables can call them, and qualify every table, because the install rewrites only `customer.` names. For the same reason their files revoke EXECUTE from PUBLIC: otherwise any role that reaches the schema (a read-only one too) could use up any organization's AI credits; a role of the assistant's own gets EXECUTE from `apps/assistant/sql/grants.sql`. An answer's price comes from the terms' tiers (by its steps) and is booked once per run (the unique index `uq_credit_usage_ai_run`), so a retried or twice-ended run is not counted twice. No credit sum of `/credits/*` includes AI credits; they come as fields and rows of their own, and the ledger never shows an answer's payload (its conversation). The interface is in `docs/billing-interface.md`.
- **Support tickets** (SaaS only): tickets in Plan4Better's Odoo Helpdesk, read and answered from GOAT (`src/core/support`). The Odoo side (the bridge user, its rights, the server action, the GOAT links in ticket emails) is the `goat_support` module in Plan4Better's Odoo.sh repository. The bridge reads no contacts, followers or ratings and posts nothing itself: all of that goes through the server action's operations, which check every input. Core and that module are released together. Who may see and do what in GOAT is decided by core alone (`service.py`); Odoo only sees the one bridge user.
- **Account reports** (SaaS with billing): verified sign-ups, new organizations with their trial and joined members are reported to a CRM through an `AccountsProvider` (`src/core/accounts/provider.py`: `sign_up`, `start_trial`, `join`, `status`). The implementation is Odoo's `goat_accounts` module in Plan4Better's Odoo: contacts and companies, through the server action "GOAT: accounts" and its bridge user (`ODOO_ACCOUNTS_API_KEY`); `accounts/factory.py` returns no provider unless billing and the Odoo settings are set. The calls run in the background after the request and never fail it; a user the provider does not know yet is reported again on a later request, and a trial, join or status report that fails flags the organization (`odoo_sync_pending`, read it as "accounts sync pending": the name predates the provider split) for a retry loop that runs every 10 minutes. With it on, the provider sends the trial emails (welcome, reminders) and GOAT only the account emails; a trial changed there comes back through `POST /webhooks/billing/entitlement`.

The full, current route list is the OpenAPI document at `/api/docs`. The liveness check is `GET /api/healthz`.

## Why It Is Built This Way

- **Metadata in core, data elsewhere.** Core never reads layer features. Layer data lives in DuckLake and is served by geoapi; analytics run in processes. Core holds only the rows that describe a layer, project or bundle, plus who may see them.
- **Background work goes through processes, never straight to Windmill.** Core submits a job with `execute_process` (`src/core/services/processes.py`) and the caller's token, so processes applies the same ownership rules as for any user request.
- **Odoo through one client, one key per integration** (support tickets, account reports). `src/core/odoo` is the only way core talks to Odoo: each integration builds its own client with its own allow-list of (model, method) calls and its own least-privilege key, and gets time limits, a concurrency cap and a circuit breaker, so a slow Odoo never ties up core. The conventions for adding an integration are in its module docstring.
- **The searches across everything a user can open start from the user's reach** (`GET /content/search`, `GET /project/layers/search`): the user's spaces, what is granted to them, their teams or their organization, and the folders below a granted folder. Only matching rows are then checked with `effective_role`, so a search never scans the installation, and a folder name matches only folders the caller can open, so a shared item never gives away the name of a private folder above it.
- **Support tickets have no background job.** The header summary is built from lists cached per user for 60 s in the process; the lists a user opens are always read fresh from Odoo, because a write on one pod cannot drop another pod's cache (only the badge may lag there). Staff replies reach users by Odoo's email. Nothing polls Odoo while nobody is active, and no pod needs to coordinate with another.
- **A template's datasets reach its users in one of three ways** (`TemplateInput.mode`, `src/core/schemas/template.py`). *Ship* links the author's layer into every project made from the template: read-only for the user unless it is also shared with them as editor. *Ask* (workflow templates) leaves a slot the user fills on use. *Copy* gives every user their own editable layer: `POST /template/{id}/use` links the author's layer like *ship*, then starts one `layer_copy` job per copied dataset through processes (`crud_template._start_copy_jobs`) and returns the job ids in `copy_jobs`. The job keeps the project entry and points it at the copy, so dashboards, layouts, filters and workflow nodes, which all name the entry, follow; until it finishes, the entry shows the author's layer. Many saved workflow nodes name their layer without an entry, so a project copy gives such a node the entry when its layer has exactly one, and the job also repoints nodes by layer while no other entry of the project shows the source. The entry's `template_copy` stamp says where the copy stands (`pending`, `done`, `failed` with the reason); a later use of a workflow template in the same project binds to that entry again, and restarts its copy if it never arrived. A copy (with rows, or empty with the same columns) is made from the layer as it is at use time: a copy frozen when the template is saved is not built yet. Ship datasets, and copies with their rows, are readable by whoever may read the template, however it reached them (a grant on it, a folder shared above it, its space): rule 6c of `effective_role` reads the template's inputs live, so that read ends when the dataset leaves the template, the template stops reaching the user, or the author may no longer share the dataset (a dataset the author may only view never travels on). Saving checks the author may read them; a template grant or a team/organisation destination also grants viewer on them (T6), and those grants stay when the dataset leaves the template; publishing makes them public. An empty copy (`TemplateInput.empty_copy`) takes only the columns, field settings and style, so its dataset stays private: no grant, never made public; the job is started for whoever may read the template, and processes runs it only for a template that has that dataset as an empty copy.
- **Style routes answer 403 and 404 themselves.** `auth_z` turns every refusal into 401, which the browser's save loop answers by renewing the session and retrying, so the style and map routes run a role check first (`access` in `src/core/endpoints/v2/style.py`): 403 when the caller may only view, 404 when the project or dataset is out of reach.
- **Layer styles are rows, saved by one statement** (GOAT Style; `src/core/services/style.py`). A project layer has up to ten named styles (`layer_style`), one of them active (`layer_project.active_style_id`); a dataset has one default style. A save sends the whole document with the revision it is based on: core merges what the author changed since then onto the stored document, has it checked (processes' `style-check` when the style reads data, goatlib's `validate` otherwise), keeps the stored value wherever a change has an error, and stores it with one conditional statement on the revision, so concurrent saves never overwrite each other; three lost races answer 503 `save_conflict`. `layer_style_revision` keeps the last 20 documents of a named style only to merge saves based on an older one: it is not a history (undo and redo stay in the browser).
- **The map is assembled, not stored** (`src/core/services/map_document.py`). `GET /project/{id}/map` builds the map document from the project's tables, because workflows, dashboards and layouts name `layer_project.id` and groups and those rows keep their ids; it carries every layer's active style and an ETag (exposed through CORS, since the web app asks from another origin with `If-None-Match` and gets `304` when nothing changed). Map state is written by small `map-state` PATCHes, last write wins. Publishing stores the map and every named style in `project_public.config`, so a published map never computes; the map request lists the layers whose styles moved on since (`unpublished`).
- **The 3D scene is one JSONB on the project, the 3D view rides in the view state.** `project.scene` (terrain, buildings, sky, fog, globe glow, light; added in Alembic `0021_layer_styles`) is saved whole through the project update (`PUT /project/{id}`, like the basemap), last write wins, after goatlib's `validate_map` checks it against the project's extruded layers and the deployment's sources (`TERRAIN_TILES_URL`, `BUILDINGS_TILES_URL`): an error stores nothing of the request (422 `invalid_scene` with the issues); warnings are not returned, since the editor prevents those combinations. NULL means automatic, so projects without a scene need no data. The map document carries it as `scene`, so a published map keeps the scene of publishing time; project copies, templates and project export and import copy the column. The view's `mode` and `projection` live next to pitch and bearing in `user_project.initial_view_state` (per user, like the rest of the view). The scene's keys are goatlib's `goatlib.style.models.scene`.
- **Styles became rows in one data migration** (Alembic `0022_layer_styles_data`, conversion in `goatlib.style.migrate`). Every dataset got its default style and every project layer a named "Default" with its revision row, converted from the old `properties`; computed values are stamped with the layer's fingerprint after the tracker ran, so the first map load recomputes nothing and a published map shows no unpublished change. Templates' remembered output styles, print layouts' locked styles and published snapshots are converted in place, their originals kept in `style_migration_undo` for `downgrade()`, which restores them and removes the rows (edits made after the upgrade are lost on the way back). The `properties` columns stay one release for going back, and the API no longer reads or returns them; during the rollout `ensure_styles` (run by the map request) gives rows to links and datasets an old pod created with `properties` only.
- **Styles name uploaded icons by id** (`asset:<id>`, never a URL), so a URL change never breaks a style. The anonymous `GET /api/v2/asset/{id}/file` redirects to an icon's or image's file (`ASSETS_URL`), so every viewer of a map draws it, public maps included; documents stay behind their key.
- **`stale` comes from DuckLake's own change log.** Before reading, the map request asks whether the log has anything new (three index reads) and only then runs goatlib's tracker statement (`advance_data_snapshots_sql`), which raises `layer.data_snapshot` past every change it has not seen yet; a style whose computed values belong to other data comes back `stale`, and reading that style alone recomputes it through processes. The DuckLake maintenance task runs the same statement before it expires snapshots, so no change expires unseen. Core reads `DUCKLAKE_CATALOG_SCHEMA`, the variable geoapi and processes use, to find the log; while that catalog does not exist yet (a fresh install before geoapi created it) the request skips the tracker.
- **A slim image.** Core installs goatlib's light base only: no GDAL, no geospatial stack. The data volume is not mounted except, read-only, the catalog subtree and the GeoIP database: core reads the catalog mirror there, and checks that a re-added catalog dataset's materialized file still exists (`src/core/services/materialize_heal.py`). Anything that needs the heavy stack (validating an upload, promoting a catalog item) runs as a job in a worker. `tests/unit/test_runs_without_the_geospatial_stack.py` guards this, after two production incidents.
- **A COG layer's extent comes from geoapi.** Core cannot read a raster file, so when it creates a raster layer from a COG address (`POST /layer/raster`) it asks geoapi's `bounds` route (`GOAT_GEOAPI_URL`, `src/core/services/geoapi.py`) as the caller and stores them as `extent`; a map service's extent comes with the request, from its capabilities. A failure stores none and never fails the creation: the web then asks geoapi for the bounds itself. geoapi only reads the layer row; layers created before core did this get theirs from a one-off command in geoapi's image (see `apps/geoapi/README.md`).
- **A workflow is saved even when it cannot run yet.** Workflows are built step by step, so on create and update core only refuses a config that cannot be read as a workflow at all (`goatlib.workflows.checks.shape_issues`: the steps and connections are lists, every step has an id and a known type, every connection has both ends) and answers 422 with the issues (`src/core/endpoints/v2/workflow.py`). Whether a workflow can run is checked by processes. A stored config is returned as it is, damaged or not, so the editor can show what is broken; duplicates, template use and project copies write configs that already exist and are not checked. `POST /template/{id}/use/preview` answers with the config a template use would write, bound the same way (`crud_template.preview_use`), and writes nothing, so a caller can have processes check it before the workflow exists. It takes the use's own rights (read on the template, write on the project); a layer the use would link anew has no `projectLayerId` in it yet, and the tool settings that name an empty copy's dataset by its entry are cleared, since the use points them at an entry it makes for the copy: the author's entry, or one whose copy has not arrived, shows the template's dataset, which the user may not read.
- **Saves of a workflow, a report layout or a dashboard through core never silently overwrite each other.** Each carries a revision (`revision`; the dashboard's is `project.builder_revision`) that reads return and every save sends back as `base_revision` (`builder_base_revision` on `PUT /project/{id}`). A save that changes the document raises it by one in the UPDATE that checks it, so one made on an older revision writes nothing and answers 409 `changed_elsewhere` with the stored revision, when and by whom it was made. A save that differs only in volatile parts (a workflow's viewport, selection and run state, and what the editor rewrites in any workflow it saves, such as the order of the steps; a layout's view toggles) is written in place without either, so an editor's autosave after panning or a run never turns another tab's next real save into a conflict, and an unchanged save writes nothing. The volatile parts are listed in `src/core/core/revision.py`. A save without `base_revision` still lands unchecked and is logged. Workers that point a workflow's dataset steps at a run's new layer (`packages/python/goatlib/src/goatlib/tools/workflow_dataset_nodes.py`) write in place without a new revision, so a run never makes an open editor's save a conflict; an editor opened before the run writes the old layer back with its next save, and the runner still reads such a step through its project entry.

## Authorization

A token counts only once it verifies: signed with the realm's key and, when `AUTH_ALLOWED_CLIENTS` is set, issued to one of those clients (goatlib's `KeycloakAuth`, see `packages/python/goatlib/README.md`). Every route that needs a login verifies it through `core.deps.auth.decode_token`, so a token of another application of the realm gets a 401 before either layer below runs.

Two layers, both in SQL:

1. **Route level.** `auth_z` calls the `authorization()` SQL function, which looks the request up in the seeded `customer.resource` table and applies the organization's quotas. **A new route needs a seeded pattern in `RESOURCES_PERMISSIONS` (`src/core/db/seed_roles.py`), or it answers 401 when auth is on.**
2. **Resource level.** `effective_role()` / `can()` (`src/core/core/authz.py`, `src/core/db/sql/functions/authz/effective_role.sql`) decide access to a layer, project, folder, bundle or template. Access can come from the space, a direct grant in `resource_grant`, an ancestor folder, a bundle, a shared project, a template that ships the layer or public read; the highest role wins. Grants stop at editor: only the resource itself makes someone its owner. A grant only reaches users, teams and the organization of the granter's own organization: every route that writes one calls `crud_share.assert_grantees_in_organization` before the row is written.

With `AUTH=False` both layers let everything through and every request runs as the built-in default user (`DEFAULT_USER_*` settings), who is seeded only in that mode.

## Gotchas

- **The engine runs in AUTOCOMMIT** (`src/core/db/session.py`). Every statement commits on its own and `rollback()` undoes nothing, so a multi-statement write can half-apply. Code that writes several rows validates everything first and orders its statements so a crash leaves an explainable state (see `crud_transfer.py`, `crud_share.py`). A check and the write it guards go in one statement: a save checks its revision in the UPDATE that writes it (`src/core/core/revision.py`). Where no order is safe, `session_manager.transaction()` gives a connection with a real transaction: the permanent organization delete takes the member out of the organization before deleting it (`user.organization_id` cascades, so the other order would delete the account), and a failure between the two must not leave an organization without its member.
- **Behind a connection pooler in transaction mode set `POSTGRES_TRANSACTION_POOLER`.** psycopg prepares a statement on the server once it ran a few times on a connection, and the next query may land on a server connection where it is missing or already taken; with the setting it prepares nothing (`engine_connect_args` in `src/core/db/session.py`, used by the migrations too). It needs the psycopg driver, the default.
- **Organization roles are not tied to an organization.** `user_role` holds only the user and the role, and a user belongs to one organization (`user.organization_id`). Accepting an invitation therefore refuses a user who already has an organization and replaces their organization roles with the invited one (`crud_role.remove_organization_roles`). Anything else that moves a user into or out of an organization must do the same (the permanent organization delete does), or an owner role carries over.
- **Migrations, unlike requests, run in one transaction** (`alembic/env.py`). Revisions 0003 and 0005 rely on that to swap tables and functions atomically.
- **Alembic starts from a squashed baseline.** `init` creates the tables from the models; functions, triggers, roles and seed data come from `src/core/scripts/initial_data.py`, which every deploy runs after `alembic upgrade head`. The numbered revisions are guarded (`alembic/helpers.py`), so they do nothing on a fresh database and still upgrade an older one.
- **A team or organization space contains what is shared into it.** `GET /content?view=space` at such a space's root lists the space's own items *and* the items other spaces granted to its team or organization, sorted and paged together (`crud_content.py`, `_space_view_sql`). Any surface that lists a space must use this one view, not combine views itself: the Content page and the dataset picker once disagreed exactly because the "shared into" part lived in the page.
- **`layer_serial_counter` is core's table but geoapi's data.** It holds the highest number each serial column ever handed out, so a deleted feature's number is never reused. Only geoapi writes it (under its per-layer advisory lock), plus two goatlib tools: the filtered-copy tool, which copies a source's rows to the copy, and `finalize_layer` when a workflow's "Save as dataset" adds rows to an existing dataset (under the same lock); the foreign key removes it when its layer is purged. Core never reads it.
- **A user's language lives in GOAT and in Keycloak.** GOAT's setting (`system_setting.preferred_language`) drives the app, core's emails and the accounts provider; Keycloak's `locale` attribute drives its own pages and emails. A new user's GOAT language starts from Keycloak's (the token's `locale`), else the browser's, else German; every change in GOAT is written back to Keycloak through the service account (`core.deps.keycloak.set_user_locale`, needs manage-users, best effort). That write sends the whole user back: with the declarative user profile an update that leaves out a field or attribute clears it.
- **SQL function files must not start with a comment** (`create_functions.py` rejects them) and are installed in dependency order.
- **Settings are case sensitive and have no prefix.** Core loads `apps/core/.env`, then `/app/.env` in containers. In a fresh checkout, link `apps/core/.env` to the root `.env` or export the variables first.

## Development

```bash
uv sync --all-packages                 # from the repo root
ln -s ../../.env apps/core/.env        # once per checkout
docker compose up -d                   # Postgres and the other infrastructure

cd apps/core
uv run alembic upgrade head && uv run python src/core/scripts/initial_data.py
uv run uvicorn core.main:app --reload --port 8000
```

Tests need a running PostgreSQL with PostGIS. Each test process creates its own schema, installs the functions and triggers into it, and forces `AUTH=False`:

```bash
cd apps/core && uv run pytest tests/ -n 4
```

## Configuration

All settings and their defaults are in `src/core/core/config.py`. The ones that change behaviour rather than just connect to something:

- `AUTH` (on unless set to false) and `KEYCLOAK_SERVER_URL`, which is required while auth is on.
- `AUTH_ALLOWED_CLIENTS`: empty accepts a token of every client of the realm; set, only tokens issued to the listed clients. Read the same way by geoapi, processes, catalog and the assistant. A list without `KEYCLOAK_CLIENT_ID` would refuse every user, so core and the assistant (which also knows the web client) refuse to start with it.
- `GOAT_PROCESSES_URL`: without it, bundle import answers 503, and a catalog dataset added to a project stays pending because its materialize job cannot start.
- `BILLING_ENABLED` (default false) and `BILLING_WEBHOOK_SECRET`: SaaS mode (billing) only when both are set: new organizations start a trial and the billing webhooks accept calls. The trial and the credit rates come from the billing system (terms push). Otherwise the `DEFAULT_QUOTA_*` values apply.
- `SMTP_HOST`: emails are sent only when it is set.
- `ODOO_URL`, `ODOO_DB` (shared by the Odoo integrations), `ODOO_SUPPORT_API_KEY`, `ODOO_SUPPORT_TEAM_ID`: support tickets are on only when all four are set. Otherwise `GET /api/v2/support/summary` answers `enabled: false`, which is how the web app learns that support is off, and every other `/api/v2/support` route answers 404. `ODOO_ACCOUNTS_API_KEY` (with the connection and billing on) switches the account reports to Odoo on.
- `ASSETS_SERVED_BY_CORE`: for a store without a publicly readable bucket. Uploaded images go to the data bucket under `assets/`, and the anonymous `GET /api/v2/asset/file/<key>` redirects to a short-lived signed URL; it signs keys under `assets/` only, never user uploads. `ASSETS_URL` then defaults to that route on `CLIENT_URL`'s host.
- `CUSTOM_DOMAIN_CNAME_TARGET`: empty turns custom domains off.
- `ALLOW_ORGANIZATION_CREATION` (default true): false lets only superusers create organizations, so nobody who signs in before their invitation sets up one of their own. The web app learns it from `GET /users/me/organization-creation` and shows a page that waits for an invitation instead of the setup form.
